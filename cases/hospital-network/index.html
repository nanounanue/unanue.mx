Positioning Medicine Across a National Hospital Network

June 2026

A public health system of national coverage — unnamed by agreement — runs sixty-four warehouses that supply 1,456 medical units. Critical medicines stocked out while others expired on the shelf: the network held inventory, but in the wrong places, and first-expired-first-out losses compounded the misallocation.

Problem

Inventory positioned by local judgment, shelf by shelf, across a network too large for judgment: shortages of critical medicines in some units coexisted with expiry losses in others.

Scale

Roughly 120 million pieces move through the network each month, across warehouses, the medical units they serve, and the supply points between them. At that size the policy has to be computed, not negotiated.

Method

Multi-echelon (s,S) and (R,S) inventory policies set by a stochastic program, with perishables handled first-expired-first-out. A digital twin replayed historical demand against every candidate policy, so service levels were measured before anything touched the field.

Result

The twin measured the service-level and inventory changes for each candidate policy; those figures are with the client for release review, and this page will carry them when they clear. What is public now is the method and the scale it ran at.
