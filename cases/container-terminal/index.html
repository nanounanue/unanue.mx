Fewer Unproductive Moves at a Container Terminal

April 2026

Most yard moves at a container terminal earn nothing: containers are dug out, re-stacked, and shuffled aside to reach others. At the terminal in the study, three of every four movements were unproductive, and about half of those traced to pre-clearance service requests — the administrative previo a customs broker asks for, distinct from formal customs inspection — that arrive with little warning (Villalobos et al. 2026).

Problem

The terminal's operating rules flagged which containers would request pre-clearance service with 25 % precision and 30 % recall — most service requests arrived as surprises, and every surprise meant digging a container out of a stack built without it in mind.

Scale

A real terminal's complete operational history, with consignees deduplicated across naming variants and cargo described by its HS classification. All figures below come from the published preprint, evaluated with temporal validation — each model tested only on months it never saw.

Method

Two supervised models: which containers will request pre-clearance service, and how long each container will dwell in the yard. Features draw on consignee history and cargo classification rather than weather-and-seasonality generalities.

Result

Service prediction reached 75 % precision and up to 80 % recall on the top-300 ranking, against 25 % and 30 % for the rules in force; the preprint estimates that placement informed by these models avoids 13 % to 23 % of total handling operations (arXiv:2604.06251, §7.5).

Simulation

That estimate is now being measured. A discrete-event simulator of the terminal's import yard, run on synthetic containers calibrated to its operations, places every arriving container under a stacking policy and logs every crane move. Each policy is compared with the rule in force on the same random arrivals, and a run counts only after database checks confirm that no container was lost, no slot held two, and no prediction changed after placement. The measured results will appear in the revised preprint.
