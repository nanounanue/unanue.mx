Positioning Medicine Across a National Hospital Network

The client — a public health system of national coverage — is unnamed by design. The numbers are real.

Problem

Sixty-four warehouses supply 1,456 medical units across the country. Critical medicines stocked out while others expired on the shelf — the network held inventory, but in the wrong places, and first-expired-first-out losses compounded the misallocation.

Scale

Roughly 120 million pieces move through the network each month, across 1,594 nodes — the warehouses, the medical units they serve, and the supply points between them. At that size no planner positions inventory by judgment; the policy has to be computed, not negotiated shelf by shelf.

Method

Multi-echelon (s,S) and (R,S) inventory policies set by a stochastic program, with perishables handled first-expired-first-out. A digital twin replayed historical demand against every candidate policy, so service levels were measured before anything touched the field.

Result

The twin measured [SERVICE-LEVEL GAIN] at [INVENTORY REDUCTION] across replications; final figures await the client’s review of the published draft. What is already public is the method and the scale it ran at.
